# Talk track

*Plain-language note: what to say when someone asks about this project. Every number matches `report/stats.json`. Update it if the numbers change after your checking.*

## 60 seconds

Community college students who pass their first college-level math class early are far more likely to finish a degree. California and Texas both changed the law in 2017 to get more students through that class, and both now report results for every college. But each state publishes differently, and none of it is linked to the federal college records researchers use.

I built the US Gateway Mathematics Outcomes Atlas to close that gap. It matches every California community college and every Texas community college or district to its federal ID, and puts their math results next to federal facts like size and the share of students with Pell grants. Version 1.0 covers 115 California colleges and 55 Texas colleges or districts.

What it shows: in California, 20.5% of the 2023 starting cohort passed transfer-level math in their first year. In Texas, 48.6% of the 2022 cohort passed a college-level math course within two years. Within each state, colleges serving similar students land far apart, and a college's Pell share barely predicts its rate. That makes the atlas useful for finding peer colleges to learn from.

It is free, openly licensed, and published with a permanent DOI so others can cite and build on it.

## Two sentences

The US Gateway Mathematics Outcomes Atlas is a free, citable resource that links state reporting on whether new community college students pass college-level math to federal college records, starting with California and Texas. It shows large differences between colleges serving similar students, which helps researchers and policymakers find peer colleges to learn from.

## Likely questions

**Can I compare California and Texas?** No. California counts one year and transfer-level math; Texas counts two years and any college-level math course. The atlas shows them side by side and never ranks one against the other.

**Does a low rate mean a bad college?** No. Rates reflect who enrolls as much as what a college does. The atlas is a starting point for asking why, not a ranking.
